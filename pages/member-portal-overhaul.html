Problem

i4cp publishes extensive workforce research and benchmarks, but members were struggling to find relevant studies. The portal's navigation had grown organically over years, ending up organized like an internal org chart rather than a member resource. If a VP of Talent wanted a compensation benchmark, they had to click through three arbitrary categories and hope for the best.

Usage logs showed the disconnect. Members would log in, navigate through cluttered categories, type keywords into legacy search, and get a wall of scattered links. While Ahri existed as a dedicated AI research tool, it was disconnected from the primary discovery flow. My job was to rebuild the navigation model around member intent, bringing Ahri and conversational synthesis directly into the front door of research discovery.

Goals

  • Organize by member intent: Map categories to how HR executives describe their business priorities rather than internal research groups.
  • Bring AI into discovery: Make Ahri the central entry point for research discovery, with a direct way to see traditional results when needed.
  • Ship a clean MVP first: Redesign the primary navigation, homepage, and search entry points without trying to rebuild the entire portal in one giant sprint.

Research

The four-tier taxonomy framework had already been established by i4cp's research leadership before I joined the project. My challenge was translating that conceptual taxonomy into an intuitive digital experience without overwhelming members.

To evaluate navigation models, I conducted moderated usability testing sessions with 5 enterprise HR executives. Testing quickly revealed the design boundaries: dumping all four tiers into a dense megamenu paralyzed members with dozens of competing links, while burying topics inside nested drill-downs meant related research studies were rarely discovered. Those sessions confirmed that progressive disclosure was the right model, exposing deeper topics only after a member selected a primary practice area.

Tier 1: Domain Areas of Focus Broad executive practice domains (Leadership, Talent, Culture, Total Rewards).
Tier 2: Intent Strategic Priorities Key business questions within each area (e.g. Executive Succession Planning).
Tier 3: Depth Subject Topics Specific subject clusters (e.g. C-Suite Turnover Benchmarks).
Tier 4: Output Content Assets Actionable research reports, risk assessment tools, and roundtable recordings.

For example, an HR leader looking for leadership succession benchmarking starts with Leadership as a broad domain, narrows into a strategic priority, scans related topics, and picks their preferred format (a research report, quick benchmark, or webinar). The navigation guides that narrowing journey without dumping the whole taxonomy on them at once.

Defining decision

Progressive disclosure over flat navigation.

Exposes too much structure at once, creating a noisy entry point for members who need a clear next step.

Reveals deeper levels only as members commit to a practice area or priority.

I explored flatter navigation patterns, including an expandable tree menu, but user testing showed it felt like browsing a dense Windows file system. Progressive disclosure won because it gives members a calm, clear starting point, revealing sub-topics only as they click deeper into an area of interest.

Constraints

Constraint

Designing During a Rebrand

An external agency was overhauling i4cp's brand guidelines at the same time, meaning colors, fonts, and styling rules were in constant flux.

Solution

Design Token Layer

I built the portal UI on a foundation of CSS variables and design tokens. When the agency handed over finalized colors and type specs, we updated them in one place without touching component markup.

Constraint

Enormous Surface Area

Changing the information architecture affected nearly every page on the platform. Trying to redesign everything at once would have delayed release by a year.

Solution

Strict MVP Phasing

We focused Phase 1 strictly on the primary navigation, global search, and the homepage. Deeper administrative views and secondary subpages were scheduled for fast follow-ups.

Post-MVP Roadmap

We intentionally postponed deeper workflow features (like automated research synthesis briefs, peer benchmarking matching, and customized alert digests) so we could ship the new navigation and discovery engine without slipping our deadline.
