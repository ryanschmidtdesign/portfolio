Leadership

Systems before screens. Polished UI doesn't matter if the underlying logic falls apart the moment engineers encounter messy edge cases in production.

At i4cp, I'm the sole product designer. I write the PRDs, maintain the design tokens, and work directly with our VP of Product and engineers to shape the roadmap. When requirements are ambiguous or data models don't match user workflows, I dig in until the team has a crisp, buildable spec.

Before this at D-Tools, I facilitated twice-weekly design reviews with five designers and six PMs to stress-test assumptions before anyone opened an IDE. The best sessions were the ones where we uncovered hidden technical friction and shifted direction before writing code.

Builder

Design in the browser. You learn ten times more about an interaction by resizing a real browser window than by clicking through static artboards.

I've always liked building what I design. With modern coding tools like Cursor, I can take a rough concept directly into working HTML and CSS to see how it actually feels. This entire portfolio was built that way.

My coding background goes back nearly a decade, building responsive web interfaces for school board tools at TASB and transit ticketing apps at Bytemark. Using AI tools today feels like a natural continuation of that. It takes repetitive typing off my plate so I can focus on product decisions.

AI doesn't supply product judgment. You still have to establish hierarchy, handle empty states, and decide what to leave off the screen. What it does give you is velocity: turning an idea into working markup in an afternoon so you can evaluate the experience directly.
